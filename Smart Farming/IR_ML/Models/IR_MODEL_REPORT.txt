================================================================================
OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Since no historical irrigation data was available, this model uses established
agricultural scientific formulas to calculate irrigation requirements. The
model is based on the FAO-56 guidelines for crop water requirements, which is
the international standard for irrigation management.

The model answers two key questions:
1. Is irrigation required? (Yes/No)
2. If yes, how much water should be applied? (in liters/meter² and total volume)

================================================================================
MODEL ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

The model implements a multi-step calculation process:

STEP 1: Reference Evapotranspiration (ETo)
- Uses simplified Penman-Monteith equation
- Factors: Temperature, Humidity, Wind Speed
- Output: ETo in mm/day (typical range: 2-8 mm/day)

STEP 2: Crop Evapotranspiration (ETc)
- Formula: ETc = Kc × ETo
- Kc (Crop Coefficient) varies by crop type and growth stage
- Different crops have different water requirements
- Growth stages: initial, mid, late

STEP 3: Effective Rainfall
- Accounts for recent rainfall
- Rainfall effectiveness decays over time (exponential decay)
- Maximum 7-day rainfall effect considered
- Factors: Rainfall amount, days since rainfall

STEP 4: Soil Water Balance
- Calculates available soil water in root zone
- Considers soil type (water holding capacity)
- Determines irrigation trigger point
- Factors: Soil moisture, soil type, root depth, depletion fraction

STEP 5: Irrigation Requirement Calculation
- Combines all factors to determine if irrigation is needed
- Calculates water deficit
- Subtracts effective rainfall
- Applies irrigation efficiency factor (75% typical)

STEP 6: Water Amount Calculation
- Converts to practical units
- 1 mm = 1 liter per square meter
- Calculates total volume for given land area
- Accounts for irrigation efficiency

================================================================================
INPUT PARAMETERS
================================================================================

Required Parameters (with defaults if not provided):

1. soil_moisture (default: 50.0)
   - Current soil moisture percentage
   - Range: 0-100%
   - Critical for determining irrigation need

2. recent_rainfall_mm (default: 0.0)
   - Amount of recent rainfall in millimeters
   - Used to calculate effective rainfall

3. rainfall_days_ago (default: 7)
   - Days since the rainfall event
   - Affects rainfall effectiveness (decay over time)

4. temperature (default: 25.0)
   - Current temperature in Celsius
   - Higher temperature = higher evapotranspiration

5. humidity (default: 60.0)
   - Relative humidity percentage
   - Lower humidity = higher evapotranspiration

6. wind_speed (default: 2.0)
   - Wind speed in m/s at 2m height
   - Higher wind = higher evapotranspiration

7. soil_type (default: 'loam')
   - Type of soil (affects water holding capacity)
   - Options: sand, loamy_sand, sandy_loam, loam, clay_loam, clay, etc.

8. crop_type (default: 'rice')
   - Type of crop being grown
   - Determines crop coefficient (Kc)
   - 27 crops supported in database

9. growth_stage (default: 'mid')
   - Growth stage of the crop
   - Options: 'initial', 'mid', 'late'
   - Affects crop coefficient

10. acres (default: 1.0)
    - Land area in acres
    - Used to calculate total water volume
    - 1 acre = 4,046.86 square meters

================================================================================
OUTPUT PARAMETERS
================================================================================

Primary Output:
- irrigation_required: Boolean (True/False)
- water_amount_mm: Water needed in millimeters
- water_amount_liters_per_sqm: Water needed in liters per square meter
- total_water_liters: Total water volume in liters
- total_water_cubic_meters: Total water volume in cubic meters
- confidence: Confidence score (0.3 to 0.95)

Detailed Output (for transparency):
- reference_evapotranspiration: ETo in mm/day
- crop_evapotranspiration: ETc in mm/day
- effective_rainfall: Effective rainfall in mm
- soil_water_deficit: Water deficit in mm
- current_soil_water: Current soil water in mm
- irrigation_trigger: Trigger point for irrigation in mm

================================================================================
CROP COEFFICIENT DATABASE
================================================================================

The model includes crop coefficients (Kc values) for 27 crops based on FAO-56
standards. Each crop has three Kc values for different growth stages:

Supported Crops:
- Rice, Maize, Wheat, Cotton, Sugarcane
- Potato, Tomato, Chickpea, Lentil, Mungbean, Blackgram
- Pigeonpeas, Soybean, Groundnut, Mustard, Sunflower
- Banana, Mango, Grapes, Apple, Orange, Coconut
- Coffee, Jute, Papaya, Pomegranate, Watermelon, Muskmelon

Example Kc Values (Maize):
- Initial stage: 0.30 (low water need)
- Mid stage: 1.20 (high water need)
- Late stage: 0.60 (moderate water need)

Each crop also has:
- Root depth (meters)
- Depletion fraction (when to irrigate)
- Maximum height

================================================================================
SOIL WATER HOLDING CAPACITY
================================================================================

Different soil types have different water holding capacities:

Sand: 60 mm/m (low water retention)
Loamy Sand: 90 mm/m
Sandy Loam: 120 mm/m
Loam: 150 mm/m (default)
Silt Loam: 170 mm/m
Clay Loam: 180 mm/m
Sandy Clay: 170 mm/m
Silty Clay: 200 mm/m
Clay: 220 mm/m (high water retention)

This affects how much water the soil can hold and how quickly it dries out.

================================================================================
LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

1. NO ML TRAINING
   - Since no historical data, uses formulas only
   - Cannot learn from past irrigation outcomes
   - No adaptive improvement over time

2. SIMPLIFIED ETo CALCULATION
   - Uses simplified Hargreaves equation
   - Full Penman-Monteith requires more weather data
   - Reasonable approximation for most cases

3. ASSUMED IRRIGATION EFFICIENCY
   - Uses 75% efficiency as default
   - Actual efficiency varies by irrigation method
   - Drip irrigation is more efficient than flood

4. NO SEASONAL VARIATION
   - Kc values are fixed per growth stage
   - Doesn't account for seasonal climate patterns
   - Should be adjusted for local conditions

5. NO PEST/DISEASE CONSIDERATION
   - Doesn't account for pest pressure
   - Disease stress may change water needs
   - Should be considered in practice
